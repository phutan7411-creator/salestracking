## Commentary

**Result.** Revenue for Jan–May 2026 reached **151.19 tỷ**, up **+4.0%** against Jan–May 2025. Service fee income came to **2.45 tỷ** (+12.4%) with profit at **4.68 tỷ** (+12.2%).

**Fee margin.** AVG SVF improved from **1.50%** to **1.62%** (+0.12 pts). Contracted fee schedules are being applied more consistently — this discipline needs to hold through renewals.

**Profitability.** AVG Profit stands at **3.10%** versus **2.87%**. Average transaction value rose to **9.0 triệu** while volume grew **+2.2%** — growth is coming from both deal size and volume.

**Concentration.** The top 5 clients account for **36.2%** of revenue, led by **BIS SAIGON** at 19.86 tỷ. The client base is reasonably diversified, which limits exposure to any single account loss.

**Booking mix.** **Local** drives **85.6%** of revenue across the 4 booking types in Com_Type 2. Online booking tool channels remain under 5% of revenue — automating high-frequency accounts onto OBT would lower cost to serve.

**Risk.** Sharp declines requiring intervention: **BIS HANOI** (-12.9%), **BVIS ROYAL** (-18.3%). An account review should establish whether this is budget contraction or competitive loss.

**Momentum.** Standout growth: **BIS SAIGON** (+15.8%), **KHACH LE** (+20.6%), **ILA GROUP** (+28.5%). These accounts are the natural targets for hotel and car rental upsell.

**Actions.** Three priorities for the second half: (1) reconcile and standardise service fees on accounts billing below contract; (2) defend Vietnam and United Kingdom, the two largest contributors; (3) grow the international ticket share, where fee margin is materially stronger than domestic.

## Domestic / International mix

| Segment | Jan–May 2026 | Share | Jan–May 2025 | Change | Tickets | Profit margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Domestic (DOM) | 33.34 tỷ | 22.0% | 35.49 tỷ | -6.1% | 10,734 | 2.60% |
| International (INT) | 117.86 tỷ | 78.0% | 109.86 tỷ | +7.3% | 6,060 | 3.24% |

**On fee schedules.** The two segments are priced on different bases: domestic tickets carry a **flat fee per segment** (typically 40–55k economy, 80–100k business), so fee income scales with ticket count and is diluted whenever fares rise. International tickets are priced as a **percentage of net fare** (around 2.0–2.7%), so income scales with fare value. That structural difference is why international business returns **3.24%** against **2.60%** domestically — a gap of **0.64 pts**. Shifting mix toward international, and repricing flat domestic fees that have not moved with inflation, are the two levers with the clearest margin impact.
